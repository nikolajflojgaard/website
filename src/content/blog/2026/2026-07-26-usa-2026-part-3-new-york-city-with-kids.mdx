This is part 3 of our USA 2026 trip.

[Part 1 was Palm Springs and Las Vegas](/posts/2026/2026-07-26-usa-2026-part-1-palm-springs-las-vegas).

[Part 2 was the Grand Canyon, Lake Tahoe, and Hawaii](/posts/2026/2026-07-26-usa-2026-part-2-grand-canyon-lake-tahoe-hawaii).

Part 3 is **New York City**.

From around **22 July onward**, the trip stopped being a road-trip-and-island story and became a city-with-children story.

Which is a different sport.

## New York does not ease you in

New York is not a gentle re-entry.

It does not whisper, "Welcome back to urban life."

It says: here are the buildings, here is the noise, here are eight hundred people crossing in different directions, and yes, your child would like a snack immediately.

The funny thing is that New York still works.

Maybe because it is so visually busy that children understand it without needing a guidebook.

Big buildings.

Weird sculptures.

Yellow cabs.

Steam, sirens, steps, glass, stone, fountains, crowds.

It is a lot, but at least it is never boring.

![New York with a reflective sculpture, because apparently even the pavement wants to be cinematic](/images/usa-2026/IMG_1006.jpg)

## The city does half the entertaining

This is the underrated advantage of New York with children.

You do not have to manufacture entertainment every hour.

The city keeps producing things to react to.

You walk a few blocks and something appears:

- a plaza
- a church
- a sculpture
- a food cart
- a fountain
- a very serious building
- a very unserious moment

For adults, that means you still get a real city trip.

For children, it means the day does not feel like one long parental lecture about architecture.

![A proper city walk, where the buildings do most of the talking and the adults just try to keep everyone moving](/images/usa-2026/IMG_0983.jpg)

## The landmarks are useful because they are obvious

Subtlety is wasted on tired family travel.

You want things that are easy to point at.

That is why New York works.

The city is full of places where nobody has to explain why you stopped.

Everyone can see it.

The building is big. The space is weird. The view is different. The sculpture looks like something from a dream someone had after too much coffee.

Done.

That counts.

![One of those New York corners where the buildings make the whole walk feel more expensive than it is](/images/usa-2026/IMG_0988.jpg)

## The Oculus still feels like science fiction

The **Oculus** is one of those places that feels like it was designed by someone who wanted a train station to have a dramatic internal monologue.

It is over the top.

But with children, over the top is useful.

They do not need to understand the architecture. They just need to look up and go quiet for three seconds.

That is enough.

![The Oculus, doing its best impression of a spaceship that also sells coffee](/images/usa-2026/IMG_0996.jpg)

## Snack logistics remain undefeated

No matter where you go, the real infrastructure of family travel is snacks.

Not museums.

Not monuments.

Snacks.

New York is good for this because something edible is almost always nearby, but that does not mean the timing is easy.

Children do not become hungry at convenient intervals.

They become hungry at the exact point where the adults have just said, "Let's just walk a little farther."

This sentence should be illegal on family trips.

It has never improved a situation.

![A small pause in a big city, which is basically the operating model](/images/usa-2026/IMG_1011.jpg)

## New York rewards low ambition

The trick with New York is the same trick as with most big cities and small children:

Lower the ambition.

Not the standards.

The ambition.

One area. One or two anchors. Lots of walking. Plenty of pauses. Snacks before anyone starts blinking slowly.

That is the whole system.

Try to do too much and the city will punish you.

Let the city do some of the work and it becomes surprisingly good.

![A cathedral stop, because even the children understand when a building has decided to be dramatic](/images/usa-2026/IMG_1015.jpg)

## The full 23-day verdict

So that was the trip:

- **Part 1:** LAX, Palm Springs, Las Vegas, and the arrival tax
- **Part 2:** Grand Canyon, Lake Tahoe, San Francisco, and Hawaii
- **Part 3:** New York City and the final urban sprint

It was not one clean vacation.

It was several different travel modes stitched together:

- pool recovery
- desert road trip
- Vegas spectacle
- canyon scale
- lake reset
- Hawaii exhale
- New York city energy

That is a lot.

Probably too much, if you write it down like a sensible person.

Luckily, we did not approach it like sensible people.

We approached it like parents with flights booked, children strapped in, snacks somewhere in the bag, and just enough optimism to keep moving.

And honestly, that worked.

Not perfectly.

Family travel never works perfectly. Anyone who says otherwise is either lying or travelling without children.

But it worked in the way that matters.

The trip produced the right kind of memories: big views, small disasters, pool days, food stops, airport shuffling, tired laughs, and that familiar moment where you look around and think:

This is completely ridiculous.

Also, I would do it again.
