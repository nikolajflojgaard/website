The first rule of travelling to the US with small children is that the children do not care about your route.

They do not care that you landed in **LAX**.

They do not care that the itinerary has drama, contrast, desert, mountains, coastline, cities, and one fairly ambitious chain of decisions made by adults who had clearly forgotten what time zones do to people.

They care about three things:

- is there a pool
- is there food
- why are we walking

Honestly, fair.

We started the trip the only sensible way: by landing in Los Angeles and immediately leaving Los Angeles.

Not because LA is bad. LA is probably great if you have sleep, patience, and a tolerance for traffic that suggests previous military training.

We had children and luggage.

So we went straight to **Palm Springs**, into a private Airbnb villa with a pool, space, sun, and the dangerous feeling that we had somehow become people who "summer" places.

![The Palm Springs pool doing exactly what a first stop after LAX is supposed to do: lower everyone's blood pressure](/images/usa-2026-first-16-days/IMG_0883.jpg)

## Palm Springs fixed the landing

Palm Springs was the correct decision.

After a long flight, the goal is not culture. The goal is survival with nice lighting.

The villa gave us the three things every family trip needs after crossing the Atlantic:

- water
- shade
- enough physical space that nobody has to negotiate every single square meter

It looked calm from the outside.

Inside, of course, it was still us.

There is no version of "private villa" that turns children into chilled resort people. They still need snacks. They still ask where everything is. They still behave like the pool is a legal obligation rather than a luxury.

But as first stops go, Palm Springs did its job beautifully.

![Palm Springs pool life, also known as the only meeting format children fully support](/images/usa-2026-first-16-days/IMG_0930.jpg)

## Las Vegas remained Las Vegas

From there we went to **Las Vegas**, which is not so much a city as a dare with hotel rooms.

Las Vegas with children is strange.

Everything is too big, too bright, too loud, too fake, and somehow still exactly what it says on the box.

The children, naturally, accepted it faster than the adults.

Adults stand there thinking, "What is this place?"

Children stand there thinking, "Why does every building look like it was designed by someone who was allowed to choose one theme and chose all of them?"

The correct cultural stop, obviously, was **Bubba Gump**.

Not because it is fine dining. Because sometimes the family travel experience is not about optimal local cuisine. Sometimes it is about sitting down, eating something predictable, and letting the adults pretend they chose the chaos on purpose.

![Bubba Gump, because every carefully planned road trip eventually becomes a shrimp-based negotiation](/images/usa-2026-first-16-days/IMG_0890.jpg)

## The Grand Canyon did not need help

Then came the **Grand Canyon**.

Some places need explanation.

The Grand Canyon does not.

You can arrive tired, slightly dusty, badly hydrated, carrying snacks you forgot were in the bag, and it still just works.

It is so obviously impressive that even children have to briefly acknowledge the scale of the thing before returning to their main research question: can I climb on this?

This was also where the trip finally got its proper cover photo.

I originally had **IMG_0944** in mind for the cover, and it is a lovely Hawaii beach picture. But for this story, **IMG_0918** is better. It has the family, the scale, and the "yes, we actually did the big American road trip thing" energy.

![The Grand Canyon doing the heavy lifting. No caption writer required, really.](/images/usa-2026-first-16-days/IMG_0918.jpg)

## Lake Tahoe was the reset button

After desert, lights, and canyon scale, **Lake Tahoe** felt like the part of the trip where the itinerary took a breath.

That is the underrated thing about this kind of route.

You need the spectacle, yes.

But you also need somewhere that does not constantly shout at you.

Tahoe gave the trip a different speed: water, trees, cooler air, and the feeling that everyone might become slightly less feral if left near a lake for long enough.

This is where family travel starts to make sense again.

Not because it becomes easy.

Because the surroundings do some of the parenting for you.

## San Francisco was mostly a launchpad

Then came **San Francisco**, but mostly as the place where the next chapter started.

Sometimes a city is the destination.

Sometimes it is the place where you repack bags, count children, find the right terminal, and try to behave like flying onwards to Hawaii is a normal logistical decision rather than a reward for surviving the previous legs.

The actual emotional beat was simple:

We had crossed a ridiculous amount of America.

Now we were going to an island.

That is not a bad sentence to be able to say.

## Hawaii was the soft landing

Hawaii is not subtle.

It does not need to be.

After LAX, Palm Springs, Vegas, the Grand Canyon, Tahoe, and San Francisco, Hawaii felt like the trip finally took off its shoes.

Beach. Palms. Water. Children who could be pointed at sand and briefly become self-operating.

That is not nothing.

![Hawaii making a very strong case for ending all family itineraries near warm water](/images/usa-2026-first-16-days/IMG_0944.jpg)

There is a specific kind of family calm that only appears near water.

It is not silent.

It is not peaceful in the spa-brochure sense.

Someone still needs sunscreen. Someone still wants a snack. Someone is always either too hot, too sandy, too wet, or personally betrayed by a towel.

But it is a better kind of chaos.

The sort where nobody is pretending the plan is more important than the day.

![Sunset in Hawaii, where even tired parents can briefly remember that this was all a good idea](/images/usa-2026-first-16-days/IMG_0966.jpg)

## The honest verdict

The first 16 days in the States were a lot.

In the best way, but still a lot.

We did not have one trip. We had several compressed into one:

- the arrival-and-pool recovery trip
- the desert villa trip
- the Vegas circus trip
- the Grand Canyon landmark trip
- the Tahoe reset trip
- the San Francisco airport logistics trip
- the Hawaii exhale trip

That is probably too much if you write it down like a sensible person.

Luckily, we did not approach it like sensible people.

We approached it like parents with flights booked, children strapped in, snacks somewhere in the bag, and just enough optimism to keep moving.

And honestly, that worked.

Not perfectly.

Family travel never works perfectly. Anyone who says otherwise is either lying or travelling without children.

But it worked in the way that matters.

The trip produced the right kind of memories: big views, small disasters, pool days, food stops, airport shuffling, tired laughs, and that familiar moment where you look around and think:

This is completely ridiculous.

Also, I would do it again.
