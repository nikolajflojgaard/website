This is part 2 of our USA 2026 trip.

[Part 1 was Palm Springs and Las Vegas](/posts/2026/2026-07-26-usa-2026-part-1-palm-springs-las-vegas): jet lag, desert recovery, Vegas volume, and Bubba Gump doing emergency diplomacy with shrimp.

Part 2 is where America stopped being airports, heat, hotel carpets, and "are we nearly there?"

It got bigger.

Then quieter.

Then wet in the expensive Pacific way.

The route was **Grand Canyon, Lake Tahoe, San Francisco, Honolulu, Waikiki, Oahu**.

The actual route was four people trying to move across a continent while **Nelly** treated every stop as a possible climbing assessment and **Jamie** operated under her own constitutional monarchy.

Janice and I were technically in charge.

Technically.

## The Grand Canyon is a ridiculous object

The **Grand Canyon** is not beautiful in the normal tourist-board sense.

It is too big for that.

Beautiful is a lake. Beautiful is a sunset. Beautiful is Janice getting five uninterrupted minutes with a coffee.

The Grand Canyon is more like someone opened the Earth and forgot to close it.

You walk up to the edge and every sensible thought leaves your head for a second.

Then the children bring you back.

Nelly wanted to know what she could stand on.

Jamie wanted the usual executive package: arms, shade, food, and immediate service.

So there we were, in front of one of the most absurd landscapes on the planet, still running the same tiny human helpdesk.

That contrast was the whole point.

The canyon did not make the trip easier. It made it feel properly stupid and properly worth it at the same time.

## Tahoe changed the volume

After the canyon, **Lake Tahoe** felt like a different country.

No neon.

No desert blast.

No geological drama trying to swallow the horizon.

Just cold-looking water, trees, rocks, air that did not feel like it had been preheated, and enough room for everyone to stop gripping the itinerary so hard.

Tahoe was not the most spectacular stop.

Good.

Spectacle gets tiring when you are doing it with a stroller.

Tahoe had the better trick: it made doing less feel like a plan.

Nelly could inspect the shoreline like a tiny municipal engineer. Jamie could exist near water, which for her is basically a full programme. Janice and I could let the day spread out instead of constantly moving it along.

That is a rare luxury on a trip like this.

Not silence.

Not serenity.

Just fewer alarms going off in the adult brain.

![Nelly at Lake Tahoe, giving the water the kind of serious review usually reserved for contracts and dessert menus](/images/usa-2026/IMG_0289.jpg)

## San Francisco was the comma

**San Francisco** was not the sentence.

It was the comma.

A pause, a transfer, a repack, a count-the-passports-and-children moment before the trip changed species again.

That sounds unfair to San Francisco, but this was not its turn. The city had one job for us: get us to **SFO** and point us across the ocean.

There is something funny about leaving a massive continental road trip and then casually adding Hawaii, as if the previous legs were merely the warm-up act.

But once "Honolulu" is on the boarding pass, nobody complains about the logic.

You just accept that the trip has become ridiculous in a new direction.

## Hawaii cheated

**Hawaii** cheated.

There is no fair comparison between "we have driven through heat and scale and mountain air" and "here is **Waikiki Beach**, please remove your shoes."

Of course that works.

Of course everyone becomes a little easier to convince when there is warm water nearby.

Not angelic.

Nobody became angelic.

But Oahu gave us a better default setting. The day could start from beach, pool, balcony, sun, food, repeat. That is not a sophisticated travel concept. It is just correct.

![Waikiki, making a strong argument against overthinking the end of a family itinerary](/images/usa-2026/IMG_0944.jpg)

The adult fantasy is that water turns children into a self-cleaning entertainment system.

It does not.

It gives you windows.

Eight minutes here. Twelve minutes there. A strange little stretch where nobody needs a negotiation, nobody is falling apart, and the only urgent problem is whether someone has sand somewhere regrettable.

You take those windows.

You do not get greedy.

![A resort pause, otherwise known as pretending this could last long enough to finish a drink](/images/usa-2026/IMG_0957.jpg)

## This was the payoff leg

Part 1 got us into the country.

Part 2 made the whole decision look less insane.

The Grand Canyon gave us scale.

Tahoe gave us breathing room.

San Francisco moved the plot.

Hawaii cashed the cheque.

![Hawaii sunset, after the part of the day where everyone needed something from someone](/images/usa-2026/IMG_0966.jpg)

That is a good middle chapter.

Not tidy.

Not smooth.

Better than that: memorable.

The kind where you can already tell which details will survive the years. The edge of the canyon. Nelly by the lake. Jamie near water. Janice and me doing that silent parent look that means "this is objectively too much" and "this is also exactly why we came."
