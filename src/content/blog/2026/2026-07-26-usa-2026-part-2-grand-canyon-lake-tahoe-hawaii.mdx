This is part 2 of our USA 2026 trip.

[Part 1 was Palm Springs and Las Vegas](/posts/2026/2026-07-26-usa-2026-part-1-palm-springs-las-vegas): pool recovery, desert heat, Vegas noise, and the very specific dignity of choosing Bubba Gump because everyone needed to sit down.

Part 2 is where the trip changed shape.

Less arrival management.

More actual landscape.

**Grand Canyon, Lake Tahoe, San Francisco as the launchpad, and Hawaii as the soft landing.**

## The Grand Canyon did not need help

Some places need explanation.

The **Grand Canyon** does not.

You can arrive tired, slightly dusty, badly hydrated, carrying snacks you forgot were in the bag, and it still just works.

It is so obviously impressive that even children have to briefly acknowledge the scale of the thing before returning to their main research question:

Can I climb on this?

That is the nice thing about the Grand Canyon.

It does not ask you to be in the mood.

It just stands there being absurd.

![The Grand Canyon doing the heavy lifting. No caption writer required, really.](/images/usa-2026/IMG_0918.jpg)

This was also the better cover image.

I first considered using **IMG_0944** from Hawaii as the hero, and it is a lovely beach photo.

But for the middle chapter of the trip, **IMG_0918** wins.

It has family, scale, sky, and the big "yes, we actually crossed a ridiculous amount of America" feeling.

That is a cover.

## Lake Tahoe was the reset button

After desert, Las Vegas, and canyon scale, **Lake Tahoe** felt like the part where the itinerary finally took a breath.

That is the underrated thing about a long road trip.

You need the spectacle, yes.

But you also need somewhere that does not constantly shout at you.

Tahoe gave the trip a different speed: water, trees, cooler air, and the possibility that everyone might become slightly less feral if left near a lake for long enough.

This is where family travel starts to make sense again.

Not because it becomes easy.

Because the surroundings do some of the parenting for you.

The best places with children are not always the ones with the most activities.

Sometimes they are just the places where the day has fewer sharp edges.

![A water day, which is the closest family travel gets to a universal reset button](/images/usa-2026/IMG_0930.jpg)

## San Francisco was mostly a launchpad

Then came **San Francisco**, but mostly as the place where the next chapter started.

Sometimes a city is the destination.

Sometimes it is the place where you repack bags, count children, find the right terminal, and try to behave like flying onwards to Hawaii is a normal logistical decision rather than a reward for surviving the previous legs.

The actual emotional beat was simple:

We had crossed a ridiculous amount of America.

Now we were going to an island.

That is not a bad sentence to be able to say.

## Hawaii was the soft landing

Hawaii is not subtle.

It does not need to be.

After Palm Springs, Las Vegas, the Grand Canyon, Tahoe, and San Francisco, Hawaii felt like the trip finally took off its shoes.

Beach. Palms. Water. Children who could be pointed at sand and briefly become self-operating.

That is not nothing.

![Hawaii making a very strong case for ending all family itineraries near warm water](/images/usa-2026/IMG_0944.jpg)

There is a specific kind of family calm that only appears near water.

It is not silent.

It is not peaceful in the spa-brochure sense.

Someone still needs sunscreen. Someone still wants a snack. Someone is always either too hot, too sandy, too wet, or personally betrayed by a towel.

But it is a better kind of chaos.

The sort where nobody is pretending the plan is more important than the day.

![A resort pause, also known as the adult fantasy of children entertaining themselves for eight consecutive minutes](/images/usa-2026/IMG_0957.jpg)

## The trip finally exhaled

The first leg was about arriving.

This leg was about actually being there.

That sounds obvious, but with family travel there is a difference.

You can spend several days technically on holiday while your nervous system is still somewhere in the airport.

By the time we hit the Grand Canyon, Tahoe, and Hawaii, the trip had settled into itself.

Not perfectly.

Never perfectly.

But enough that the big moments could land.

![Sunset in Hawaii, where even tired parents can briefly remember that this was all a good idea](/images/usa-2026/IMG_0966.jpg)

This was the part of the trip that made the whole route feel worth it.

Big view.

Quiet water.

Warm island ending.

Children still negotiating with reality, obviously.

But in better surroundings.
